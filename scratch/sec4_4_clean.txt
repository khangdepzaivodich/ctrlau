To enforce relationships that are truly causal rather than merely correlational , we introduce a Feature-level Counterfactual Intervention (CF) module. The core principle is that if a source node genuinely exerts a causal influence on a target, then intervening on the source should induce a meaningful change in the target’s representation and prediction, whereas intervening on non-causal nodes should produce little to no effect. We realize this via a thresholded soft mask with edge-aware saliency, which perturbs source node embeddings conditioned on the graph. By contrasting model behaviors under factual and counterfactual conditions, the model receives consistency and discrepancy signals that guide graph refinement, reinforcing informative edges and suppressing spurious ones.
